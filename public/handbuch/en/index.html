    Chapter 01

    Your first tank in a few steps

    Ready to get started? This short route covers the essentials. The other chapters explain each feature in more detail.

    What you can do here

    Tank
    All records belonging to one tank are kept together here.
    Readings and log
    Readings record values; the log records completed work.
    Add more later
    You can use photos, sharing, AI and Social later as needed.

    Step by step

    1. Sign in and create your first tank with a name, water type and size.
    2. Add animals and plants from the catalogs. Deliberately choose any relevant forms or L-numbers.
    3. Open “New measurement”, choose your test methods and save your first readings.
    4. Record the next care task in the log. Then set a suitable measurement cycle.
    5. Open your profile for language, appearance and test usage. For offline use, first wait for the initial online synchronization to finish.

    Chapter 02

    Sign in and get started

    You need the address of your aquarium app and a user account. You can read this guide without signing in.

    What you can do here

    Sign in
    Your account opens your own tanks and tanks shared with you. Use the same account on each visit.
    Register and verify email
    Registration creates an account. Email verification confirms that the address belongs to you.
    Invitation or external sign-in
    An invitation allows registration on restricted servers. Pocket ID is an alternative sign-in method configured by the operator.

    Step by step

    1. Open your app address in a browser. Sign in with your username or email address and password.
    2. No account yet? Choose “Register” if registration is enabled. Enter the required information, then read and accept the terms of use.
    3. Confirm your email address using the link you receive. If no message arrives, check your spam folder and ask the operator to check email delivery.
    4. If you have an invitation, open its link first or enter the invitation code during registration.
    5. If your operator has configured Pocket ID or another OpenID Connect provider, you can use the external sign-in option.

    Forgot your password?

    Use the password reset option on the sign-in page and open the link in the email. This requires configured email delivery. Never share your password or confirmation links.

    Chapter 03

    Your overview

    After signing in, you see your aquariums. Click the logo at the top left to return here at any time.

    What you can do here

    Tank cards
    Each card represents a tank. Tap it to open its readings, residents and care records.
    Owned and shared tanks
    You manage your own tanks. A shared tank’s permission determines whether you can only read or also change records.
    Language and appearance
    The header lets you switch between German and English and between light, dark and system appearance.

    Step by step

    1. “My aquariums” shows tanks you own. “Shared with me” contains aquariums whose sharing invitations you have accepted.
    2. Open an aquarium by selecting its card. You will find current readings, warnings, care cycles and the other sections there.
    3. The header links to species, plants, health, Social, notifications and your profile.
    4. Use the language button to switch between German and English. The theme button selects a light, dark or system theme.
    5. When online, the app synchronizes data with the server automatically. The local data timestamp and “Synchronize now” appear only offline. They disappear when the server is reachable again. Pending changes and errors remain visible for review.

    Why do some sections appear only when scrolling?

    Charts, residents, plants and the logbook load progressively. Scroll to the section you need. If loading fails, you can retry that section.

    Chapter 04

    Create or edit an aquarium

    An aquarium keeps measurements, animals, plants, photos and care activities together.

    What you can do here

    Name, water type and size
    These fields describe your tank. Water type and size help put readings and animal care into context.
    Description and cover image
    A motto, text and photo make multiple tanks easier to distinguish. They do not replace readings or care records.
    Edit and delete
    Editing corrects tank details. Deleting removes the tank and its associated records and requires deliberate confirmation.

    Step by step

    1. Choose “+ New aquarium” in the overview. Enter a name that helps you recognize the tank.
    2. Choose freshwater or saltwater and enter its volume in liters. Add a motto, description and notes if needed.
    3. Check the details and save the aquarium. It will appear in your overview.
    4. To make changes, open the aquarium and choose “Edit”. You can also adjust care cycles using “Configure cycles” in the tank view.
    5. Only delete an aquarium when you no longer need its data. Read the confirmation and export or back up important data first.

    Water type and residents belong together

    The water type affects available species, plants and assessments. Before changing it, check whether the existing residents are suitable.

    Chapter 05

    Remember the next water test

    The measurement cycle shows when you plan to check the water again. It does not take readings automatically.

    What you can do here

    Interval
    This sets how often you want to test, for example every seven days.
    Due or overdue
    The status compares the planned date with the current time.
    Plan doses separately
    Fertilizers have their own plans in the fertilizer area. A measurement cycle is not a dosing plan.

    Step by step

    1. Open the tank and choose “Configure cycles” beside the measurement cycle.
    2. Choose an interval in days or weeks. Select a weekday where offered. An interval of zero disables the cycle.
    3. Enable an email reminder if needed, then save. This requires a verified address and working email delivery.
    4. Read the next due date on the cycle card. “Measure now” opens the usual measurement form.
    5. Save a test you actually performed. Check the updated due date and change the cycle if your routine changes.

    Chapter 06

    Measure and save water parameters

    You can record individual readings or a complete set. Empty fields are not saved as measurements.

    What you can do here

    Choose a test method
    Choose the test you actually use. The selection determines the timers offered and which test inventory receives the usage.
    Start a timer
    A timer reminds you of a test’s reaction time. It does not perform the test or enter its result. The additional Active timers FAB appears only while timers are running. Expand it to see remaining times grouped by tank, stop a timer or open its test. Open tests and Active timers collapse each other.
    Save readings and time
    Only completed values are saved. An earlier timestamp is useful when recording a test you performed before opening the app.
    Pause tests for each aquarium
    Values, test selection, measurement time with its original time zone, notes and timer deadlines are stored locally for each account and aquarium and synced with your other devices when connected. The expandable “Open tests” button lists these drafts. “Pause test” returns to the overview; reloading and closing preserve drafts already saved locally. Signing out hides them; signing in again with the same account restores them. “Save measurement” creates the measurement and closes its draft together; “Discard test” removes the draft after confirmation on all devices once synced.

    Step by step

    1. Open your aquarium and choose “New measurement” or “Measure now”. Check the measurement time.
    2. Open “Customize water tests” and select the tests you actually use. You can combine methods from different manufacturers.
    3. “Apply suite” replaces your selection with that test suite. “Add suite” extends it; one method remains selected for each water parameter.
    4. Follow the instructions supplied with your water test. Start the timer next to a parameter if needed. Several timers can run at the same time.
    5. Enter the result you read. A decimal comma is accepted. The color indicator helps you interpret the value.
    6. Choose “Pause test” to return to the overview and measure at the next aquarium. Expand “Open tests” at the bottom right and select an aquarium to resume. Starting another test for the same aquarium asks whether you want to return to its open test.
    7. To switch devices, wait for pending changes to sync while connected and sign in with the same account on the other device. Open “Open tests” there. Offline you can continue previously downloaded drafts; let local saving finish before closing the app.
    8. If differing entries are reported, expand the local changes and choose “This device” or “Other device” for each affected field. Select “Apply selected values”; equal values and independent additions need no decision.
    9. Add a note if needed and choose “Save measurement”. The reading appears in the aquarium history and logbook.

    Timers and notifications

    Choose “Enable” to allow browser notifications. Sound, vibration and keeping the screen awake depend on your device. Keep the app visible for important reaction times; phones may suspend background processes. Expired timers offer “Stop timer” and “Open test”. Stopping removes only the timer and preserves entered values. Opening resumes the corresponding test and automatically pauses another open test. If the matching test is already open, the view stays unchanged. System notifications offer these actions where supported by the browser; clicking the notification also opens the test in its original tab.

    Changing methods during a measurement

    Changing the test method may discard a running timer and an unsaved value for that parameter. Choose the method before starting the test whenever possible.

    Parameter and unit

    A parameter is a measured quantity, such as temperature or pH. Its unit appears beside the field, for example °C or mg/l. Use the unit and result of your actual test; different units are not interchangeable without conversion.

    Offline and simultaneous editing

    Changes remain in the account-specific queue even offline. Devices sync on reconnect, when returning to the app and normally every 30 seconds while visible. If one device adds pH and another adds nitrate, both readings are merged automatically. Equal numbers also match with decimal commas, decimal points or extra trailing zeroes. The starting point distinguishes unchanged entries from intentional corrections and clearing a field. Only conflicting entries require a choice for each affected field: “This device” or “Other device”. Make each choice and select “Apply selected values”; other additions are preserved. Simultaneous changes to notes, measurement times, methods and timers are also protected from silent overwrites. Closed or replaced test sessions are never restored automatically. Read-only shares do not expose drafts; users with write access each have their own tests. Timers keep their original deadlines. Mobile background notifications depend on the device.

    Chapter 07

    Set up and calibrate a YINMIK meter

    Electronic meters need appropriate preparation. The wizard guides you through the selected model; you perform every action on the actual meter yourself.

    What you can do here

    Model-specific steps
    Seven meter families are available as test suites. The wizard shows procedures for the selected parameters and identifies button sequences missing from public sources.
    Before each use
    The short procedure covers samples, stable readings, units and cleaning. A completed checklist is not a calibration certificate. In an active test, Calibrate meter saves locally, pauses the form and reopens it afterwards. Completing the full procedure shows “You did it!” with confetti; reduced motion keeps the message without animation.
    Read offline
    After a complete online sync, guides and the wizard are available offline. Manufacturer links require internet. Readings are entered manually.

    Step by step

    1. Open test selection in your profile or under New measurement. Choose the exact YINMIK model and add individual parameters or the entire suite.
    2. The wizard opens. Compare model, buttons and probe with the supplied manual; stop if they differ. Prepare the specified standards and clean containers.
    3. Perform each step on the meter. Confirm it only after a successful check; use the exact model manual for missing button instructions. Back lets you review previous steps.
    4. After calibration, check a fresh portion of the standard. Wait for stable sample readings and observe entry units; for example, 3.5% salinity equals 35 ppt.
    5. Close the wizard and enter readings. In an active test, choose Calibrate meter directly on a measurement card. The test is saved locally and paused. After the last confirmed step, confetti and “You did it!” appear. The test automatically reopens after this brief celebration; cancelling also returns without losing readings, notes or running timers. The meter button in test selection also opens the wizard.

    Matching standards and probe

    Do not interchange pH buffer families. S-200 uses 3.5% salinity standard, not EC standard. Do not enter oxygen saturation percentages as mg/l. Conditioning, assembly, electrolyte, polarization time and storage depend on your exact probe.

    No remote device control

    The app does not connect through Bluetooth or verify hardware. Completing the wizard does not calibrate other meters. If readings are unstable or the check measurement fails, stop measuring and follow the device manual for troubleshooting.

    Chapter 08

    Understand charts, tables and warnings

    Current readings appear near the top of the aquarium. The history shows how your water changes over time.

    What you can do here

    Latest readings
    The tank shows the latest available reading for each parameter. Check its date: an old reading may not describe today’s conditions.
    Charts and target ranges
    The line shows changes over time. Highlighted ranges help compare readings with stored limits; they do not explain the cause of a deviation.
    Table and warnings
    The table shows individual readings and notes. A warning highlights a deviation; acknowledging it only records that you have seen it.

    Step by step

    1. Scroll to “History” in the aquarium. Choose a range such as seven days, three months, one year or all time.
    2. For your own date range, choose “Custom” and set the start and end dates.
    3. Compare the curve with the target range and marked critical limits. A single reading does not explain what caused a deviation.
    4. The measurement table lists dates, values and notes. More rows load when needed.
    5. Warnings appear in the aquarium and under “Notifications”. Check the reading and the conditions in your tank before acknowledging a warning.
    6. With sufficient permission, you can delete an incorrectly recorded measurement. This does not restore the water test already consumed.

    Colors provide guidance

    Assessments depend on water type, stocking profiles and available thresholds. They do not replace a professional assessment of your aquarium.

    Chapter 09

    Record care in the logbook

    Use the logbook to record work on your tank. Measurements appear there too.

    What you can do here

    Record care work
    A log entry records work such as filter care, feeding or pruning. This helps explain later changes.
    Water change percentage
    The percentage describes how much water you replaced. In a 100-litre tank, 30 percent is about 30 litres.
    Filter and correct activities
    The combined timeline distinguishes care work from readings. With write permission, you can edit or delete incorrect care entries.

    Step by step

    1. Choose “New logbook entry” in the aquarium. Set the time and type of work.
    2. For water changes, enter the percentage of water replaced. Describe other work in the appropriate fields.
    3. You can document water changes, filter maintenance, plant trimming, new plants, cleaning, fertilization, feeding and other activities.
    4. Save the entry. In “Aquarium logbook & activities”, filter by activity type or load older entries.
    5. You can edit or delete care entries in your own aquariums or tanks shared with the appropriate permission.

    Set up reminders

    Use “Configure cycles” to set measurement and fertilization intervals, such as every seven days or on a specific weekday. The app shows due tasks. Email reminders require enabled reminders and configured email delivery.

    Chapter 10

    Manage fertilizers and dosing plans

    The app records fertilization and reminds you of planned work. It does not control a physical dosing pump.

    What you can do here

    Manual fertilizer
    The plan reminds you when to dose. Confirm only after adding the fertilizer; confirmation creates the corresponding log entry.
    Dosing pump plan
    An automatic plan records scheduled doses. The app does not operate or monitor a physical pump.
    Amount, interval and pause
    Millilitres specify the dose and the interval specifies how often it occurs. Pausing stops future plan actions without deleting the history.

    Step by step

    1. Scroll to “Fertilizers” in the aquarium. Choose “+ Manual fertilizer” or “+ Dosing pump”.
    2. Enter a clear name, the fertilizer, the amount in milliliters and the interval. Fill in the available time and reminder options.
    3. For a manual fertilizer, confirm a dose you have actually administered using the provided button. This creates a logbook entry.
    4. For automatic pump schedules, the app records due doses according to the plan. Check your physical pump independently.
    5. Edit or pause a plan when your fertilization changes. Removing a plan leaves existing logbook entries intact.

    Chapter 11

    Photos and gallery

    Gallery photos belong to your aquarium. They are not automatically published in the public Social feed.

    What you can do here

    Gallery and cover image
    The gallery collects photos for a tank. A cover image helps you recognize that tank.
    Resident photo and avatar
    A resident photo belongs to an animal or group. Your avatar belongs to your user profile. Each has its own upload field.
    Private or published
    A gallery photo does not automatically become a public post. For Social, deliberately create a separate post and choose its visibility.

    Step by step

    1. Choose “+ Upload photo” in the aquarium. PNG, JPG, WebP and GIF are supported up to the displayed five-megabyte limit.
    2. Choose your own image and add a caption. You can set it as the aquarium’s cover photo.
    3. Open a gallery image to view it at full size. You can change captions and the cover photo later.
    4. With sufficient permission, you can delete images you no longer need.
    5. Upload a resident portrait in the residents section. Change your own avatar in your profile.

    Chapter 12

    Import and export measurement data

    CSV is a simple table format. Use it to transfer data or analyze it in a spreadsheet.

    What you can do here

    Export
    Export downloads readings as a table. It is useful for your own analysis but does not contain all app data such as photos or accounts.
    Map columns
    During import, tell the app which column contains a date, reading or note. The preview shows how it understands the file.
    Check an import
    Check timestamps, units and decimal values before importing. Importing a file again can create additional records.

    Step by step

    1. Choose “CSV export” in the aquarium to download measurements. The semicolon option works well with Excel; it creates a CSV file, not an XLSX file.
    2. Choose “CSV import” to bring in existing measurements. Upload a file or use the provided text field.
    3. Check the delimiter and whether the first row contains column headers.
    4. Map each required column to a field such as date, time, pH or note. Ignore columns you do not need.
    5. Review the preview before starting the import. Read any notices about invalid or skipped rows.
    6. After importing, check the history and table. Imported readings are assessed and may count toward test consumption.

    Before large imports

    Keep the original file and back up important existing data first. Intentionally importing the same file again is a new action; synchronization deduplication does not replace checking your import selection.

    4. If appropriate, add the species to an aquarium from its details or open the linked health information.

    Chapter 14

    Animals and compatibility

    Add animals through the species catalog whenever possible so their care requirements can be associated with them.

    What you can do here

    Add from the catalog
    The catalog entry connects a resident to care information and the health area. The selected form receives its own stock entry.
    Group or individual animal
    The quantity represents a group. Individual names let you track animals separately, for example a pleco named “Goldie”.
    Compatibility and custom entries
    Hints compare known requirements. Custom entries may lack catalog information; no warning is not a guarantee of suitable care.

    Step by step

    1. Open “Species catalog” and search for the species. Check its water type, tank volume and group requirements.
    2. Choose “Add to aquarium” from the parent entry. Select a documented form or variety in “Form / subspecies”, or leave it unspecified. Then enter the quantity; individual animals can have names. The Ancistrus card also offers documented L-numbers. “Separate species” and “Undescribed / unidentified species” are not subspecies; check their own parameters in the selection hint.
    3. In the aquarium, you can also add custom residents, change quantities and names, or upload a portrait. “Form / subspecies” identifies the selected form, including individually named animals and offline.
    4. For known species, “Species details” opens their information again. You can also start the diagnosis assistant from a resident.
    5. Read compatibility guidance on group size, tank size, behavior and requirements. Custom entries without a catalog match have less automatic information.
    6. Remove animals that are no longer in the tank so assessments and thresholds reflect the current stock.

    Browse the species catalog

    Filter by water type and category or search by name. Viewing an image does not share your aquarium. Sources and image credits are listed in the species details.

    Chapter 15

    Choose cultivated forms, plant cultivars and L-numbers

    A shared card can contain several selectable entries. A trade name or L-number is not automatically a scientific subspecies.

    What you can do here

    Cultivated form or cultivar
    This is a selectively bred appearance, such as a particular color or leaf shape.
    Separate species
    Sharing a selection card does not make a separate species a subspecies of another.
    L-number and synonyms
    L-numbers identify particular loricariid catfish. Several numbers may find the same entry; not every L-number catfish belongs to Ancistrus.

    Step by step

    1. Search the appropriate catalog for a form, for example “Pangolino”, “Gold” or “L183”. The parent card identifies the matching entry.
    2. Open “Add to aquarium”. Choose the tank and then the form. For bristlenose catfish, the field is “Species / L-number / form”.
    3. Read the selection hint. Separate Ancistrus species have their own parameters; the shared card’s base parameters do not apply to every L-number.
    4. Enter the quantity and save. The tank keeps the actual selection visible, including for individually named animals.
    5. If identification is uncertain, do not invent a subspecies. Use the unspecified base entry or a suitable custom entry and clarify the identification.

    Chapter 16

    Find plants and add them to a tank

    The plant catalog helps you find suitable plants and record the stock in your aquarium.

    What you can do here

    Look up plants
    Details help with lighting, placement, growth and care. The catalog also includes labeled moss and algae entries.
    Choose a cultivar
    Cultivars such as Pangolino and Pinto appear under their shared card. Select one when adding; it remains identifiable in the tank.
    Change total quantity
    The quantity is the total stock of that plant in the tank. Changing three to five means five in total, not eight.

    Step by step

    1. Open “Plants” in the header. Filter by water type or category, or search for a species or cultivar name. Forms appear under a shared parent card; “Matching forms” lists the forms found by your search.
    2. Open a plant and read its placement, lighting, growth and care guidance.
    3. Choose “Add to aquarium”, select a suitable aquarium and, if offered, the specific form in “Form / subspecies”. Enter the quantity and save. The selected form keeps its own stock entry. In the aquarium, “Form / subspecies” also identifies the selected form, including offline.
    4. In the aquarium’s plant section, you can change quantities or remove plants.
    5. To record trimming or planting in your history, add a separate logbook entry for that work.

    Chapter 17

    Diseases, diagnosis and treatments

    The health section provides reference information and a question-based assistant. It cannot provide a definitive veterinary diagnosis.

    What you can do here

    Disease catalog
    It explains typical signs, possible causes and further information. Search and filters help you find relevant entries.
    Diagnosis assistant
    The assistant asks questions and ranks possible causes. It does not examine the animal and cannot replace a professional diagnosis.
    Look up treatments
    Linked treatments and supplies provide further information. A match is not a recommendation to medicate immediately.

    Step by step

    1. Open “Health”. Filter the disease catalog by water type, resident type or search term.
    2. Open an entry and read its symptoms, causes, guidance, sources and associated treatments.
    3. To use the diagnosis assistant, select its tab or launch it from an aquarium or resident.
    4. Choose the aquarium and resident. Answer the questions as accurately as possible. Relevant current water readings may inform the assessment.
    5. Read the list of possible conditions, the information it uses and the limits of the result. Seek professional help if you are unsure or symptoms are serious.
    6. Under “Medications & supplies”, search for products and read their details, warnings, sources and legal information.

    Offline diagnosis and AI are separate

    Once the required catalogs and aquarium data have been downloaded, local diagnosis can work without a connection. An optional follow-up AI chat requires a connection and configured AI access.

    Do not medicate based only on a list of matches

    Read the entry’s guidance, product instructions and professional advice. An association shown in the catalog is not an individual treatment recommendation.

    Chapter 18

    Start the diagnosis assistant

    First choose the affected animal or group. This is preselected if you start from the residents section.

    What you can do here

    Tank and resident
    The selection determines which animal and water type your answers refer to. Individual names and stored forms help identify it.
    Yes, no or unknown
    Answer only what you have observed. “Unknown” is better than a guess and is considered in the result.
    Interpret the result
    The list provides guidance. Consider recent readings, natural coloration, care conditions and behavior together.

    Step by step

    1. Check the aquarium and resident selection.
    2. Start the questionnaire and answer each question in turn.
    3. Review the result together with water readings, husbandry conditions and animal behavior. You can restart the assistant with updated information.

    Chapter 19

    Look up medications and supplies

    This catalog supplements disease details. It does not replace professional advice.

    What you can do here

    Medication or supply
    This area contains information about treatments and supportive measures. Read the details instead of deciding by product name alone.
    Warnings and other residents
    Consider fish, shrimp, snails and plants. The same treatment can have different suitability for different residents.
    Sources and use
    Sources show the basis of the information. Follow the instructions for the actual product and veterinary advice where appropriate.

    Step by step

    1. Open “Health”, then “Medications & supplies”.
    2. Search by name or narrow down the list using the available filters.
    3. Open the medication details. Pay particular attention to warnings, restrictions, sources and legal information.
    4. Consult a knowledgeable professional or veterinarian where needed; do not apply a treatment based solely on a search result.

    Chapter 20

    Set up the optional AI assistant

    AI is optional. You can manage your aquariums without your own AI access.

    Work on your phone and without a connection

    With a reachable server, the open app synchronizes data. Offline, you can see the local data timestamp and try to synchronize manually.

    What still needs a connection?

    New sign-ins, account security, AI, invitations and sharing, Social and administration require a connection. Catalogs are available offline only after downloading. Your operating system may suspend a closed or background app.

    When two devices change the same data

    The app shows a conflict. Compare local and server data. “Keep local version” tries to save your version against the new baseline; another conflict is possible. “Discard” removes the local action and, where applicable, dependent actions. Independently recorded entries from different devices remain separate.

    Local data is not a backup

    Pending changes exist only on this device and are not included in server backups. Browser data can be deleted. Synchronize regularly. Sign in again after an account change if another tab has changed your session.

    Chapter 31

    Understand pending changes and conflicts

    Changes recorded offline wait on this device. For open water tests, the app compares individual entries: additions and equal readings merge automatically. Conflicting changes require your choice.

    What you can do here

    Pending
    The change is saved locally but has not been confirmed by the server.
    Conflict
    The same data changed since your starting point, so it is not simply overwritten.
    Discard or apply
    You decide what happens to the pending local action. That decision is not a backup.
    Merge individual readings
    The sync compares the starting point, your entry and the other device’s current version. You can decide for each conflicting field while retaining other readings. Changes to different parameters and equal numbers do not trigger unnecessary prompts.

    Step by step

    1. Open the list of locally saved entries when synchronization is pending. Check which action is waiting.
    2. Reconnect and keep the app open. Offline, you can use “Synchronize now”.
    3. For a conflict, read the local and server versions. Decide which data is still correct.
    4. For water tests, choose “This device” or “Other device” only for the displayed differing fields and click “Apply selected values”. One-sided additions and equal numbers need no decision.
    5. “Apply local version” retries your change against the new starting point. Another conflict is possible if the record changes again.
    6. “Discard” removes the local action; dependent actions may also be removed. Independent entries stay separate.
    7. Before switching devices or clearing browser data, wait for synchronization to finish. For an error, also check current write permission.

    Chapter 32

    Online features when disconnected

    Some features need the server, including Social, sharing, account security and AI. If it cannot be reached, a clear offline notice appears.

    What you can do here

    Offline notice
    The notice means the request cannot reach the server. You can continue using local tank data that is already available.
    Check now
    This button retries the paused request. It is different from synchronizing tank actions already saved locally.
    Close
    Closing ends this attempt. It does not store the online request as a task for later.

    Step by step

    1. Check your Wi-Fi or mobile connection. Even with Wi-Fi connected, the app server may be unreachable.
    2. Tap “Check now”. The app retries the same request. Without a connection, the notice stays open; with a connection, the action continues.
    3. Use “Close” to cancel the request and continue using local aquarium data. Open the feature again later to start a new attempt.
    4. If the connection broke while saving, check whether the action already succeeded before retrying. Otherwise, repeating it could create duplicate changes.

    No automatic online actions

    These requests are not stored persistently and are not replayed automatically when reconnecting. Changing the view or signed-in account discards the pending retry. Locally queued aquarium actions still synchronize through their separate offline queue.

    Chapter 33

    Updates without unexpected reloads

    A new app version does not necessarily mean you need to reload the interface.

    What you can do here

    New interface
    A prepared interface update can be activated with “Load now”. This reloads the tab, so save unfinished input first.
    Continue for now
    “Later” lets you continue in the open version. Once all previous tabs are closed, the browser can activate a waiting update normally.
    New catalog data
    Species, plant and health changes are loaded separately. These data changes do not need an interface update notice.

    Step by step

    1. If frontend files change, “New version available” appears. Finish ongoing measurements and save your input.
    2. Wait for the update to be prepared if necessary. “Load now” activates the prepared build and reloads this tab.
    3. Choose “Later” to keep working. Another new build may be offered again.
    4. Changes only to species, plant or health data synchronize separately. They do not require a frontend update dialog.
    5. If an update fails, restore the connection and retry. Other open tabs may still use their previous build.

    Catalogs are not downloaded on every click

    Existing offline catalogs are normally checked at most every twelve hours. Failed catalog synchronization can be retried. New data appears in the relevant views after successful synchronization. After the catalog-reading logic changes, the app checks existing catalogs earlier once. Open form selections update after successful synchronization; your selection and input are preserved.

    Chapter 40

    Administration: back up and restore

    Backups protect data already saved on the server. Pending offline actions on other devices are not included.

    What you can do here

    Create a backup
    A backup contains the saved server state. Depending on the options, it includes images and encryption; pending device actions are absent until synchronized.
    Automatic backups and retention
    A schedule creates backups regularly. Retention determines which older backups are kept.
    Restore
    Restoring replaces the server data. Keep the password and server encryption key safe and stored separately.

    Step by step

    1. Open “Backups” in the admin area. Create a manual backup and choose the available image and encryption options.
    2. Download important backups and store them separately from the app server. Back up the server’s shared app encryption key separately as well.
    3. For automatic backups, set the interval, retention and available password and file options.
    4. Before restoring, all users should synchronize pending offline actions. Also create a current backup of the existing server state.
    5. Choose only a trusted backup and read the confirmation. Restoring replaces the server’s data; it does not simply append individual rows.
    6. After restoring, check sign-in, aquariums and images. Missing or incorrect keys and passwords can make encrypted data unreadable.

    Backups are confidential

    Backups may contain account, aquarium and image data. Do not share them publicly. Local automatic backups alone do not protect against loss of the entire server.

    Chapter 41

    When something does not work

    These checks often help without deleting any data.

    What you can do here

    Permission or connection
    Missing permissions and an unreachable server are different problems. Read the message in the affected area first.
    Try again
    Reload a failed section or use “Check now”. Before sending again, check whether the action already arrived.
    Ask for help
    State the action, area and exact error. A sanitized screenshot helps; credentials should never be included.

    Step by step

    1. A button is missing: check that you are signed in and own the aquarium or have write access. Some features must be enabled by the operator.
    2. A view does not load: check the connection and use “Retry” in that section. Do not delete browser data while offline actions are pending.
    3. An image or catalog is missing offline: open the app online and wait for synchronization to finish. Not every online feature works offline.
    4. An email is missing: check your spam folder and ask the operator to review SMTP, the queue and logs.
    5. A timer gives no notification: check notification permissions, volume and battery-saving mode. Keep the app visible for important reaction times.
    6. A local action remains pending: read the error or conflict, check your permission and deliberately choose whether to keep or discard the local version.
    7. You need support: note the affected section, action and exact error message. Redact personal information and never disclose passwords or API keys.
